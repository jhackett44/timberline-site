How Often Does a Restaurant Hood Suppression System Need to Be Inspected in Oregon?

4 min read
Published by Timberline Fire Systems
Portland, Oregon

If you own or operate a restaurant in Oregon, your kitchen hood fire suppression system is not optional equipment and neither is its maintenance schedule. Here's what the law requires, what the inspection covers, and what happens if you skip it.

The short answer: every six months

Oregon adopts NFPA 96, the Standard for Ventilation Control and Fire Protection of Commercial Cooking Operations, as its governing standard for commercial kitchen fire safety. Under NFPA 96, all fixed fire suppression systems protecting commercial cooking equipment must be inspected and serviced at least every six months.

That means twice a year, every year, without exception.

Who enforces this?

Enforcement comes from a few directions. Your local fire marshal can cite you during an inspection. Your insurance carrier can deny a claim if your suppression system wasn't properly maintained at the time of a fire. And in Portland specifically, the Portland Fire and Rescue Code Compliance division actively enforces commercial kitchen fire safety standards.

Important

The inspection must be performed by a technician holding a valid Certificate of Fitness for Fixed Fire Extinguishing Systems issued by the Portland Bureau of Development Services. An active and compliant CCB license is mandatory for technicians as well. This isn't a job for a general handyman or your hood cleaning company.

What does a semi-annual inspection actually cover?

A proper inspection per NFPA 96 includes a full review of all system components. Here's what a thorough technician checks on every visit:

  • Visual inspection of all nozzles and nozzle caps for blockage or damage
  • Fusible link inspection and replacement
  • Agent cylinder and pressure gauge check
  • Expellant cartridge inspection
  • Pull station and manual actuation device test
  • Hood and duct clearance verification
  • Fuel and electrical interlock inspection
  • Full system service history review

At the end of the inspection you receive a signed report documenting every finding. That report is what you show your insurer, your fire marshal, and your health inspector.

What about fusible links specifically?

Fusible links are the small heat-sensitive components that automatically trigger your suppression system when temperatures get high enough. They are single-use devices and must be replaced at every semi-annual inspection, not just inspected.

Under Portland Fire Code 904, fusible links must be replaced annually at minimum. Since inspections happen twice a year, they get replaced at each visit. This is one of the details that separates a thorough inspection from a rubber stamp.

What happens if you skip an inspection?

A few things can happen, none of them good. Your fire marshal can issue a citation and require immediate compliance. Your insurance carrier can use lapsed maintenance as grounds to reduce or deny a payout after a kitchen fire. And if a fire occurs and your system fails to activate properly, the liability exposure is significant.

Bottom line

The cost of skipping an inspection is almost always higher than the inspection itself. A proper semi-annual inspection is one of the lowest-cost, highest-protection investments a restaurant operator can make.

Food trucks too?

Yes. Food trucks operating in Oregon are subject to the same NFPA 96 requirements as fixed commercial kitchens. If your food truck has a suppression system protecting your cooking equipment, it needs to be inspected on the same semi-annual schedule.

Portland's food truck community is one of the most active in the country, and suppression inspection is one of the most commonly overlooked compliance requirements for mobile operators. Don't let it catch you off guard at permit renewal time.
