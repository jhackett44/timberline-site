Backflow assembly testing for the Portland metro.

Your water provider requires your backflow assembly to be tested every year. We test it, send the report, and tell you plainly if something needs attention.

Why it matters

Protecting the water supply.

A backflow assembly keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the public drinking water supply. If water pressure drops, a cross-connection can pull contaminated water back into the clean supply. These devices are built to stop that.

Water providers require them wherever your plumbing could create that risk. Any property with a fire sprinkler system must have its assembly tested every year. The test has to be done by a state-certified tester.

Scheduling the test is your job as the owner. The water provider doesn't do it for you.

Annual testing

What's included

  • Test of each backflow assembly on your property
  • Check of shutoff valves, test cocks, and visible condition
  • Written test report on the form your water provider requires
  • Report sent to your water provider within their deadline
  • Retest after repairs
  • Referral to a repair provider if an assembly fails
  • Reminder when your next annual test is due

How testing goes

  1. Find your assemblies. We locate each one and note the make, model, and size.
  2. Test. We test each assembly with calibrated gauges. Water to that part of your building is off for a short time.
  3. Report. You get a copy and we send one to your water provider.
  4. Retest if needed. If an assembly fails, we tell you what failed. Once it's repaired, we retest and file the passing report.

Who needs it

  • Fire sprinkler lines
  • Irrigation systems
  • Boilers
  • Water reuse systems
  • Anything else your water provider flags
  • Not sure if you have one? Look for a metal assembly near your water line with test valves on it, or ask us and we'll help you find out.

If an assembly fails

A failed test doesn't mean you're in trouble. It means something inside needs attention.

We don't do repairs, but we can refer you to someone who does.

Don't wait. In Portland, an owner whose assembly fails and isn't fixed promptly can be held responsible for any illness or injury that results.

Common questions

Backflow FAQ

How often do I need a backflow test?

Once a year, and again after any repair.

Who has to schedule it?

You do. The water provider doesn't do the testing.

Do you send the report to the city?

Yes. In Portland, testers must submit reports within 10 business days.

Does the test shut off my water?

Briefly, for the part of the building the assembly protects.

Do you cover Beaverton and Hillsboro?

Yes. Each water provider has its own rules, so we follow the one that serves you.
