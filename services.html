Hood suppression

Kitchen hood suppression inspection and service.

We inspect and service fixed fire suppression systems in commercial kitchens. That means the wet chemical system over your fryers, griddles, ranges, and charbroilers, plus the pull station, gas shutoff, and piping that go with it. We also service dry chemical kitchen systems. We work on major brands including Ansul, Badger, PyroChem, and Amerex.

01
Inspection

Semi-annual inspection

The fire code requires suppression systems in commercial kitchens to be inspected every six months, and again after any activation. We test the full system and give you a signed report on every finding.

A typical single-hood kitchen takes about an hour. Larger kitchens take longer. We work around your schedule so your line stays running.

What's included
  • Visual check of all nozzles and blow-off caps
  • Fusible links inspected and replaced
  • Agent cylinder weight, pressure gauge, and expellant cartridge check
  • Pull station and manual actuation test
  • Fuel and electrical shutoff test (gas valve and appliance interlocks)
  • Check that your cooking line still matches the system design
  • Hood and duct clearance check
  • Review of service history and tag
  • Updated service tag and signed report
02
Service

System service

Inspection tells you what's wrong. Service fixes it. We handle standard service items on the same visit when we can.

We'll walk you through what needs replacing and why before we do anything. Service items are billed in addition to the inspection fee.

What's included
  • Fusible links replaced at every service visit
  • Nozzle cleaning and new blow-off caps
  • Expellant cartridge replacement as needed
  • Agent cylinder recharge after a discharge
  • Pull station cable and linkage service
  • System reset and operational test
03
After an activation

After a discharge

If your system goes off, call us. The fire code requires service after any activation, and your kitchen shouldn't cook until the system is back in working order.

We inspect the system, recharge it, replace what needs replacing, reset it, and update your tag.

What's included
  • Full system inspection after discharge
  • Agent cylinder recharge
  • Replacement of fusible links, cartridges, and blow-off caps as needed
  • Check of fuel and electrical shutoff
  • System reset and operational test
  • Updated tag and signed report for your insurer
04
Mobile kitchens

Food truck inspections

Food trucks in Portland follow the same suppression rules as fixed kitchens. Mobile kitchens just have tighter spaces and different access, so you want a technician who's comfortable working in them.

We work directly with Portland's food truck community and know the mobile food unit permitting process.

What's included
  • All standard inspection items
  • LP gas or electric shutoff inspection
  • Hood and cooking equipment clearance check
  • Signed report formatted for Portland mobile food unit permitting

How an inspection goes

  1. Book a time. Call or email. We'll work around your prep and service hours.
  2. Walk-through. We look at your cooking line and check it against how the system was designed.
  3. Test and inspect. We test every part of the system without discharging it.
  4. Report and tag. You get a signed report and an updated tag before we leave.

What we look for

  • Missing or damaged blow-off caps
  • Fusible links loaded with grease
  • Pull station blocked or hard to reach
  • Cooking equipment moved or added since the system was installed
  • Gas or electric shutoff that doesn't trip
  • Expired or missing service tags
  • Older systems that may not meet current listing standards (see our UL 300 article)

If we find a problem

We tell you in writing, in plain language. You get a list of what we found, what code it relates to, and what it takes to fix. Many items we can fix on the same visit.

If a system can't work as designed, we tag it as impaired and notify you in writing. Cooking equipment shouldn't run while the system is out of service. We'd rather say that clearly than hand you a passing report.

Fire extinguishers

Portable fire extinguisher service.

Every commercial kitchen needs working portable extinguishers, including a Class K for grease fires. We service them, tag them, and sell new and replacement units.

05
Extinguishers

Annual service and sales

NFPA 10 requires a quick check every month and a full maintenance exam at least once a year by a certified technician.

Your staff can handle the monthly check. We handle the annual exam and tag every unit. When we're already on site for your suppression system, we can service your extinguishers on the same visit.

What's included
  • Annual maintenance exam per NFPA 10 and the manufacturer's service manual
  • Check for damage, corrosion, and blocked nozzles
  • Pressure gauge, weight, and pull pin check
  • New tamper seal and updated service tag on every unit
  • Flag any 6-year internal exams or hydrostatic tests that are due
  • Class K extinguisher and placard check for commercial kitchens
  • Sales of new and replacement extinguishers, including Class K
  • Written record for your files

How a visit goes

  1. Book a time. Tell us how many units you have and where.
  2. Walk the building. We find every extinguisher, including the ones behind the walk-in.
  3. Service each unit. We inspect, test, and tag every extinguisher.
  4. Report. You get a record of every unit and anything that needs attention.

What the code expects

  • A Class K within 30 feet of your cooking equipment. Measured by walking path, not a straight line.
  • Monthly checks. Anyone on your staff can do these, at least every 31 days.
  • Annual maintenance. A certified technician, once a year.
  • Staff who know how to use them.
  • Portland enforces the 2018 edition of NFPA 10.

What we look for

  • No Class K near the fryers and range
  • Units blocked, moved, or on the floor
  • Missing or broken tamper seals
  • Gauges outside the green zone
  • Dents, corrosion, or damaged hoses and nozzles
  • Missing or out-of-date service tags
  • Units due for a 5- or 6-year internal exam

Backflow testing

Backflow assembly testing.

A backflow assembly keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the public drinking water supply. Water providers require them wherever your plumbing could create that risk, and they have to be tested every year by a state-certified tester.

06
Backflow

Annual backflow testing

We test your assemblies, file the report, and tell you plainly if something needs attention. Already booked with us for hood or extinguisher service? Ask us to add backflow to the same visit.

What's included
  • Test of each backflow assembly on your property
  • Check of shutoff valves, test cocks, and visible condition
  • Written test report on the form your water provider requires
  • Report sent to your water provider within their deadline
  • Retest after repairs
  • Reminder when your next annual test is due

How testing goes

  1. Find your assemblies. We locate each one and note the make, model, and size.
  2. Test. We test each assembly with calibrated gauges. Water to that part of your building is off for a short time.
  3. Report. You get a copy and we send one to your water provider.
  4. Retest if needed. If an assembly fails, we tell you what failed. Once it's repaired, we retest and file the passing report.

Who needs it

  • Fire sprinkler lines
  • Irrigation systems
  • Boilers
  • Water reuse systems
  • Anything else your water provider flags
  • Not sure if you have one? Look for a metal assembly near your water line with test valves on it, or ask us and we'll help you find out.

If an assembly fails

A failed test doesn't mean you're in trouble. It means something inside needs attention.

Don't wait. In Portland, an owner whose assembly fails and isn't fixed promptly can be held responsible for any illness or injury that results.

Common questions

Backflow FAQ

How often do I need a backflow test?

Once a year, and again after any repair.

Who has to schedule it?

You do. The water provider doesn't do the testing.

Do you send the report to the city?

Yes. In Portland, testers must submit reports within 10 business days.

Does the test shut off my water?

Briefly, for the part of the building the assembly protects.

Do you cover Beaverton and Hillsboro?

Yes. Each water provider has its own rules, so we follow the one that serves you.
